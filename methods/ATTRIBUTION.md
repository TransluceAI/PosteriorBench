# Attribution

The code in this repository is released under the MIT License (`LICENSE`). Most of its methods were published by others. This file names, for each method, the paper it comes from, the authors' code, and what this repository takes from that code. Code adapted from another repository is in the GFlowNet family, the messages that ask a writer LLM for candidate prompts adapt Bloom's, and the rubrics and the rubric judge's prompt of two tasks are WeirdChat's; the three licenses are in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES/`.

The method data is not part of this repository (README, "Method data"). Its candidate prompts were written by DeepSeek-V4-Flash with `prompt write-candidates`, which follows Bloom (see the paragraph on the candidate prompts below).

The original model of every benchmark task is Qwen3.5-0.8B; the tasks of `generalization/` also use Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-31B-it. A run downloads a model from the Hugging Face Hub under that model's own license. No weights are included here.

Writing the candidate prompts (`prompt/writer_messages.py`, `prompt/candidate_writing.py`) follows Bloom (Gupta et al. 2025, "Bloom: an open source tool for automated behavioral evaluations"; `safety-research/bloom`, MIT, Copyright (c) 2025 Safety Research), compared with commit 39c2649. The three stages are Bloom's, and the messages to the writer LLM adapt the prompts of `bloom/prompts/step1_understanding.py`, `step2_ideation.py` and `step3_rollout.py`: the system messages of the first two stages are Bloom's, the first paragraph of the third is, and the other messages keep Bloom's structure, tags and counting instructions around text of our own. The docstring of `prompt/writer_messages.py` lists what we changed. License: `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES/safety-research-bloom-MIT.txt`. The rest of the code that writes candidates is our own.

Building the steering vectors (`steering/construction.py`, `steering/refusal.py`, `steering/coherence.py`, `steering/construction_prompts.py`) is our own code and follows the pipeline of Angell et al. (`rangell/LMTailRisk` at commit b9e383136fdeeb441d56f93744774dcafd6e039b, no license). This repository includes nothing of that pipeline. A reader makes a checkout of it, and at run time the code here imports from it the search for the refusal direction (`refusal_direction/pipeline/submodules/generate_directions.py` and `select_direction.py`) and the hooks that ablate the direction (`refusal_direction/pipeline/utils/hook_utils.py`), and reads from it the instructions of the search (`refusal_direction/pipeline/model_utils/model_base.py`, `refusal_direction/dataset/splits/`) and the prompt of the coherence judge (`coherence_0_100` in `persona_vectors/eval/prompts.py`). The checkout's folder `refusal_direction/` is a copy of `andyrdt/refusal_direction` (Arditi et al. 2024) with an Apache 2.0 license file, and its folder `persona_vectors/` a copy of `safety-research/persona_vectors` (Chen et al. 2025, Apache 2.0). The construction prompts of the paper's vectors, which are method data, were written by gpt-oss-120b with the prompt `generate_trait` of `safety-research/persona_vectors`; that prompt is not included here either. `tests/steering/lmtailrisk_stand_in/` is a stand-in of our own with the interfaces of the checkout, for the tests.

The generalization tasks (`generalization/`) are our own code. Their two LLM-judged behaviors, the rubrics by which they are judged (`generalization/data/rubrics.json`: the transcript rubrics `direct-kill-yourself-transcript-v1.1` and `false-physical-embodiment-transcript-v1.9`), the prompt of the rubric judge and the prompt of the review (`generalization/judge.py`) come from WeirdChat (Chowdhury et al. 2026), which is work of our own group. The rubrics are those of WeirdChat's dataset (`Transluce/WeirdChat`, `data/rubrics.parquet`) and the prompt of the rubric judge is the template of `weirdchat/judge.py` in its repository (`TransluceAI/WeirdChat`, commit 8dc004d); both are MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Transluce, and are included here under that license (`THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES/TransluceAI-WeirdChat-MIT.txt`). The prompt of the review is not part of WeirdChat's public release; it is included here with its authors' permission. The original models of these tasks are Qwen3.5-0.8B, Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-31B-it, and the judge models of the paper are Gemma-4-31B-it and GPT-6 Sol; a run downloads the weights or calls the servers under their own terms, and none are included here. The method data of these tasks is not part of this repository either. Its candidate prompts were written by DeepSeek-V4-Flash with `prompt write-candidates`. The construction prompts of the steering vectors of the two LLM-judged behaviors were written from the behaviors' rubrics with the prompt `generate_trait` of `safety-research/persona_vectors`, by gpt-oss-120b for physical embodiment and by DeepSeek-V4-Flash for direct encouragement; the vectors of the four tasks on the larger models use the construction prompts of the benchmark's behaviors.
